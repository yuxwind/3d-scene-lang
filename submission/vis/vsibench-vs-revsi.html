VSI-Bench vs ReVSI

ReVSI asks VSI-Bench's questions again on corrected 3D annotations, two more datasets and 13 question types. It drops appearance order and the two-way left/right question, adds "farthest", "facing away", two-category counting and a "main room" size question, and changes a few words in every template.

VSI-Bench: test.jsonl (all 5,130) and test_debiased.parquet. ReVSI: the 64-frame split (64_frame/test-00000-of-00001.parquet), the one used for the Claude run. Both read on gandalf by scripts/analysis/bench_compare.py (repo c13ebdc).

1

What each benchmark asks, by task

  • ReVSI has no appearance-order task (618 VSI-Bench questions, 12 %). Its overall score averages 7 tasks, VSI-Bench's 8.
  • ReVSI splits four tasks into sub-types: counting (single / two categories), relative distance (closest / farthest), relative direction (forward / backward × easy / hard), room size (main room / whole scene).
  • Absolute distance and object size are about a third of each benchmark (35 %); route planning and room size stay small in both (4–6 %).
2

How the question wording changed

  • Every template changed slightly: "this room" became "the scene", distance became "direct distance", and object size starts with "Based on visual evidence from the video".
  • VSI-Bench's medium direction question (left / right / back) is ReVSI's forward easy. VSI-Bench's two-way easy question is gone; ReVSI adds the same questions facing away from the object (backward).
  • ReVSI's quadrant question drops VSI-Bench's sentence explaining the quadrants (44 → 23 words).
  • ReVSI uses finer object names from its own annotation (e.g. "double-bowl drainboard kitchen sink", "standing fan", "wall picture").

3

Options and answers

  • Option counts match type by type: 4 for relative distance and quadrants, 3 for left / right / back, 2 for VSI-Bench's left / right, 3 or 4 for route planning.
  • VSI-Bench spreads the correct letter evenly (within ±1 per letter). ReVSI does not: in forward easy the answer is B 37 % of the time and A 29 %.
  • The correct direction is skewed in both. "Back" is the answer to 5 % of VSI-Bench's left / right / back questions and 0.8 % of ReVSI's forward ones. The quadrant answer is front-left or front-right 83–88 % of the time (back-left or back-right for backward questions). A model that never says "back" loses little.
  • ReVSI's routes have more turns: 57 % ask for two turns (VSI-Bench 42 %), and only 17 % for one (VSI-Bench 39 %).

Numeric answers

  • ReVSI's distances are longer: the shortest is 1.0 m (VSI-Bench 0.4 m) and the median 2.75 m (1.8 m).
  • Its objects are a little smaller (median 82 cm vs 96 cm) and its rooms smaller (median 12.5–12.8 m² vs 17.5 m²), with ARKitScenes making up most of the single-room questions.
  • Counting with one category looks the same; the two-category questions have larger answers (median 5, up to 81).

5

Where the scenes come from

  • VSI-Bench: 288 scenes from ScanNet, ARKitScenes and ScanNet++. ReVSI: 380 scenes, adding 3RScan and MultiScan; ARKitScenes is 41 % of its questions.
  • VSI-Bench's appearance-order questions come only from ScanNet and ScanNet++ (32 scenes). VSI-Bench also has a debiased subset (2,362 questions); ReVSI has none.
6

Do the two benchmarks ask the same questions?

  • They share 222 scenes: all 48 of ReVSI's ScanNet++ scenes, 121 ARKitScenes and 53 ScanNet scenes. 3RScan and MultiScan are new.
  • Only 317 of VSI-Bench's 5,130 questions (6 %) are asked again in the same scene, all numeric: counting, object size, absolute distance and room size.
  • No multiple-choice question repeats. ReVSI picks new reference objects, new distractors and finer object names for relative distance, and new start, facing and target objects for relative direction and route planning.
  • Where a question repeats, ReVSI's corrected annotation often changes the answer: counts mostly go up, sizes change (a trash bin 98 → 60 cm), room areas shift by about 3 %.
  • So the ground-truth scene is near-perfect on both for another reason: each benchmark computes its answers from its own 3D boxes, and Claude gets those boxes. Distance, size, count and direction are exact geometry on them.

7

Why route planning scores higher on ReVSI

  • Route questions name a start, a facing object, waypoints and a destination. In VSI-Bench, 83 % of route questions name at least one object that is not in the scene list Claude gets; in ReVSI, 42 %.
  • The VSI-Bench list is VSI-Bench's own meta_info boxes, the ones its other questions were generated from. They cover only the categories those questions use, so doors (45), sinks (39), windows (29), walls (26), cabinets and closets are absent. Some scenes list only sofa, toilet while the route passes a sink, a heater and a doorframe.
  • ReVSI's list is its full corrected annotation (objects seen in the 64 frames). What it misses is mostly structure (room door, window, wall) or objects outside those frames.
  • Claude does better when every named object is in the list, on both benchmarks: 81 % vs 63 % on VSI-Bench, 91 % vs 84 % on ReVSI. VSI-Bench simply has many more questions where Claude must guess the route.
  • Whether VSI-Bench's turn answers disagree with the geometry is not settled. A simple walker that follows the route on the boxes matches ReVSI's answer 72 % of the time (5 % with left and right swapped, so the frame is right), below Claude's 88 %; ReVSI's turn rule must differ from the walker's. On VSI-Bench only 21 questions have every object, too few to judge.

8

What this means for the Claude results

  • Claude Opus 5.5 with the ground-truth scene and no code: 93.7 on ReVSI (7 tasks), 82.7 on VSI-Bench (8 tasks, mean of 3 appearance-order reruns).
  • Most of the gap is appearance order, which ReVSI doesn't have and which a scene list cannot answer (44.6). Without it, VSI-Bench's 7 tasks average 88.1.
  • Route planning is far higher on ReVSI (88.2 vs 58.8) despite ReVSI's routes having more turns; section 7 shows most VSI-Bench routes name objects missing from the list. Room size is weak on both (68.4 vs 62.6).
